FP16 generally preserved accuracy. The earlier diagnostic precision study, which used different runtimes for different precisions, found mean losses of 0.08\pp for FP16 and 21.08\pp for INT8. These describe accuracy, not comparable GPU speedups. EfficientNet-B0 p0 lost 69.16\pp in that diagnostic INT8 test; its TensorRT INT8 accuracy was also poor, at 13.59\%. Engine inspection confirmed integer compute: p0 INT8 integer-layer counts were 16/26/54/105 for ResNet-18/34/50/101, 54/64 for MobileNet Small/Large, 82 for EfficientNet and 57 for ShuffleNet. Thus, successful INT8 execution did not necessarily produce a usable classifier.

\textit{Calculating cost.} Latency and energy are separate objectives, not one combined score. End-to-end latency is $1000T/N$ ms/image, where $T$ is the sum of timed batch durations in seconds and $N$ the image count. GPU energy is $(E_{\rm end}-E_{\rm start})/N$ mJ/image from NVML's cumulative mJ counter; average power is energy in joules divided by elapsed seconds. Five repeats are averaged for reported costs. Policy calibration uses prior candidate-only static costs at the matching batch size, not full routed costs; the latter include router and dispatch overhead. Costs are measured, not inferred from pruning ratio, parameter count or MACs.

\pagehead{2. Earlier routing tests and the change in method}
\textit{Screening} was a preliminary accuracy check, not a learning method. Each pruned model was compared with its own unpruned model (p0) to see whether its accuracy loss stayed below 0.5, 1, 1.5 or 2 percentage points. For example, MobileNetV3-Small p10 scored 60.82\% against p0's 62.44\%: a loss of 1.62 points, so it passed the 2-point check but not the 1.5-point check. Its MAC count was 27.11\% lower. No pruned ResNet, EfficientNet or MobileNet-Large passed even the 2-point check. This left nine source models, or 27 precision configurations.

That shortlist was abandoned as a restriction on routing. A model can have lower overall accuracy yet correctly classify images that another model gets wrong. All 213 configurations therefore remain selectable. The original screening used historical test accuracy and is only descriptive; current policy selection uses validation data. The rejected pruning and quantization results remain recorded.

The earlier scalar routers output one score: the cheapest correct candidate's cost rank, normalized by candidate count minus one, then mapped to a model by calibrated thresholds. Methods included a gradient stump, depth-3 tree, logistic regression, depth-2 XGBoost and TinyCNN; logistic used a binary rank-related target. Image-tree scores used mean or percentile aggregation. In the October 1 study, \emph{correctness} outputs instead estimated each candidate's success, while \emph{advantage} outputs estimated candidate correctness minus p0 correctness: +1 for correcting a p0 error, -1 for introducing one, and 0 when both were correct or both wrong.

\pagehead{3. Model complementarity and correctness-router training}
For seeds 42/45/48, split the 5,000 classifier-validation images into 3,000 router-fit and 2,000 calibration images. ``Validation-only'' describes the supervision source, not absence of training. The 10,000 test images evaluate the frozen policy, never tune it. The validation set had already informed classifier checkpoint selection, a limitation.

The validation-selected reference, ResNet-101 p0 FP32, obtained 85.88\%. An oracle that knows the correct label and can choose per image obtained 99.28\% over all 213 candidates: only 36 of 5,000 images were wrong for every model. Restricting this oracle to the eight unpruned FP32 models gave 95.68\%. These are upper bounds, not measured learned-router performance. MobileNet-Large p0 FP16 corrected 250 reference errors but made 441 errors on images the reference got right; EfficientNet p0 FP16 corrected 246 and introduced 383. Their lower total accuracy therefore does not imply that their predictions are redundant.

\textit{Targets and outputs.} Run each candidate on a labelled training image $x$: $y_m(x)=1$ if its predicted class equals the true label, otherwise 0. For three candidates with outcomes wrong/right/right, the target is $[0,1,1]$, not one exclusive model label. The CNN takes a 16$\times$16 thumbnail and produces 213 logits $z_m$; sigmoid converts each to estimated correctness $p_m=1/(1+e^{-z_m})$. These are not class probabilities and need not sum to one. Training minimizes mean binary cross-entropy $-[y\log p+(1-y)\log(1-p)]$ over images and candidates, for 20 Adam epochs at learning rate 0.001. At inference, true labels are unavailable. The exported batch router returns 213 mean correctness probabilities; the policy then chooses one classifier:
\[
q_m(B)=|B|^{-1}\sum_{x\in B}\sigma(z_m(x)),\qquad
m^*(B)=\arg\max_{m\in\{1,\ldots,213\}}\left[q_m(B)-\lambda\frac{C_m(B)}{\max_j C_j(B)}\right].
\]
Here $q_m(B)$ is model $m$'s mean predicted correctness and $C_m(B)$ its measured static cost. Calibrate $\lambda$ separately for latency and energy at allowances 0.5/1/1.5/2\pp. The accuracy constraint applies to the policy, not each candidate: a lower-overall-accuracy model remains selectable for batches where it is predicted to succeed.

Three fits and six objective/seed calibration groups completed. Each seed has six fixed-shape TensorRT engines, including the true final batch of 16. Maximum PyTorch/TensorRT differences were $2.3842\times10^{-7}$ for seeds 42/45 and $1.7881\times10^{-7}$ for seed 48. Times cover fitting, export and calibration, not classifier training. Lower fitting loss alone does not establish test accuracy or savings.

Correctness supervision already existed in the earlier 10-candidate benchmark; this study expands it to 213 choices. Related work includes input-based selection in PERTINENCE \cite{pertinence} and Taylor et al. \cite{taylor}, benefit-of-computation targets in DynamicDet \cite{dynamicdet}, CARN \cite{carn} and adaptive-inference policies \cite{adaptive}.

\textit{Reading the results.} The \emph{accuracy reference} is validation-selected ResNet-101 p0 FP32, with 85.80\% test accuracy. ``Accuracy pass'' means $85.80-A_{\rm routed}\leq\delta$, where $\delta$ is the row's allowance in percentage points. Thus, 84.02\% loses 1.78\pp: it passes 2\pp, but fails 1\pp. Passing does not imply speed or energy savings.

The \emph{static alternative} uses one fixed classifier, without routing. For each seed/objective/batch/allowance, choose the cheapest candidate meeting the p0-relative calibration accuracy requirement, using prior static costs. Freeze its identity before testing, then measure it afresh. Reported L/E savings use this alternative, not necessarily p0 FP32:
\[
\text{saving (\%)}=100\frac{\text{static alternative cost}-\text{routed cost}}{\text{static alternative cost}}.
\]
A +20\% means 20\% less time or GPU energy; negative means more. This validation-selected comparator is not a proven runtime optimum. Positive savings count as accuracy-compliant only if the row also passes.

However, 67 tests failed the accuracy allowance, and no latency setting both passed and improved on its comparator for all three seeds.

Timing includes RGB transfer, GPU normalization, the router, selection, engine loading/eviction, inference and synchronization. Decode/resize and batch pinning are excluded equally from both paths. The example routers spent about 1.30--1.34\,ms/batch on the GPU decision component. \textit{Jetson.} The study is paused with 75/213 engines built and accuracy-validated, 74/213 fully measured static targets, and 2,232/6,390 energy repetitions. EfficientNet and MobileNet-Large account for 60 targets/1,800 repeats; MobileNet-Small p40 INT8 has 12/30 repeats saved, ending at batch 16, repeat 1. Three architectures are staged; five still need transfer. The last saved target reached 54.375$^{\circ}$C. No new global Jetson routing has run. Detailed target results remain on the paused device; the local update uses its verified checkpoint counts. Engines must be built with Jetson TensorRT 8.6.2, not reused from the PC.

\textit{Next experiments.} The eight classifiers used the same 45,000 training and 5,000 validation images, with no unused reserved pool. More router supervision would require either explicitly exploratory predictions on classifier-training images, or slower classifier retraining/out-of-fold predictions. This choice is still pending. Seven additional correctness routers are prepared: depth-1/depth-3 trees, logistic heads, depth-2 XGBoost (32 trees per candidate), and TinyCNN inputs 8/16/32. The current smaller CNN remains a separate baseline. Eleven CPU tests passed in 15.699\,s; real-data GPU fitting, export and runtime evaluation are unfinished.

The proposed first comparison uses seed 42, batches 32/128, latency/energy objectives and 1/2\pp allowances, with one full-test repeat: 56 configurations, or 64 with a newly trained current-CNN baseline. Full confirmation would add multiple seeds and five repeats, giving 840 additional tests (960 including the existing 120). No parameters will be selected from the completed test results. Per-configuration numbers and source hashes remain in \code{numerical\_summary.json}; full repetitions are retained locally in \code{numerical\_results.json}.
